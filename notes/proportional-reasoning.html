01

Start with the foundations

Teach these words before introducing formulas.

Quantity and units

A quantity is something we can count or measure: books, students, rupees, centimetres, litres or hours.

A unit tells us how it is measured: cm, m, g, kg, ml, L, minutes or hours.

Rule: convert quantities to the same unit before forming a ratio. For example, 1 m : 50 cm = 100 cm : 50 cm = 2 : 1.

Factors and HCF

A factor divides a number exactly. Factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12.

The HCF is the largest common factor. HCF(12, 18) = 6.

12 : 18 = 2 : 3

Teaching move: ask pupils to list common factors, circle the largest, then divide every ratio term by it.

What is a ratio?

A ratio compares quantities by division. If there are 2 red counters and 3 blue counters, red : blue = 2 : 3.

  • Read 2 : 3 as “2 is to 3”.
  • Order matters: red : blue = 2 : 3, blue : red = 3 : 2.
  • Write the quantities in the same order as the question.
  • For a ratio with several terms, compare all the quantities, e.g. apple : mango : orange = 3 : 2 : 5.

Equivalent ratios and simplest form

Multiply or divide every term by the same non-zero number to make an equivalent ratio.

2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 6 : 9

To simplify 20 : 16 : 12 : 8, divide each term by 4 to get 5 : 4 : 3 : 2.

Common mistake: adding or subtracting the same number from ratio terms does not generally produce an equivalent ratio.
02

Proportion: are two ratios equal?

Use equivalent ratios and cross-multiplication to check.

Two ratios are proportional when they have the same value.

a : b = c : d ⇔ a × d = b × c
  1. Write the ratios in the same order.
  2. Multiply diagonally (cross-multiply).
  3. Compare the products. Equal products mean the ratios are proportional.

Try it: 2 : 3 and 4 : 6

Left cross-product: 2 × 6 = 12

Right cross-product: 3 × 4 = 12

⚖️

12 = 12, so the ratios are proportional.

Use positive quantities in the usual school examples. Cross multiplication is a checking method, not a substitute for reading the question carefully.

03

Divide a whole quantity in a ratio

Use this reliable 3-step method for money, fruit, students and mixtures.

One part = Total ÷ Sum of ratio parts
Share = (that person's parts ÷ total parts) × Total

Worked example: ₹600 in the ratio 2 : 3

  1. Add the parts: 2 + 3 = 5.
  2. One part = ₹600 ÷ 5 = ₹120.
  3. First share = 2 × ₹120 = ₹240.
  4. Second share = 3 × ₹120 = ₹360.
  5. Check: ₹240 + ₹360 = ₹600.

04

Representative fraction (RF): ratios on maps

Map distance is smaller than real-world distance, but the ratio is fixed.

An RF of 1 : 50,00,000 means 1 unit on the map represents 50,00,000 of the same unit in reality.

Actual distance = Map distance × Scale factor

Worked example

Scale = 1 : 50,00,000; map distance = 4 cm.

  1. Actual distance = 4 × 50,00,000 = 2,00,00,000 cm.
  2. Convert cm to km: 1 km = 100,000 cm.
  3. 2,00,00,000 ÷ 100,000 = 200 km.
Keep the units the same while multiplying. Convert to kilometres only after finding the actual distance in centimetres.

From the uploaded chapter: the worked example uses RF 1 : 50,00,000 and 4 cm to obtain 200 km.

05

Pie charts and sector angles

A pie chart shows how a total is divided into categories.

The key rule

Sector angle = (Category value ÷ Total) × 360°

A complete circle is 360°. Each category gets a share of those 360 degrees.

The moving circle is illustrative; calculate each angle from the data given in the question.

Worked example

15 of 40 students prefer football.

  1. Fraction = 15/40.
  2. Angle = 15/40 × 360°.
  3. Angle = 135°.

How to draw it

  1. Find the total of all values.
  2. Calculate each sector angle.
  3. Draw a circle and mark the centre.
  4. Use a protractor to mark each angle in order.
  5. Label and colour each sector.

Chapter-style example: favourite sports

Football 15, Cricket 9, Basketball 6, Tennis 6, Hockey 4 students. Total = 40.

06

Direct proportion

Both quantities increase or decrease together in the same ratio.

Same direction

Assuming the unit rate stays constant, more items cost more money; more time at a constant speed means more distance.

y = kx  or  y ÷ x = k

k is the constant of proportionality: the fixed amount per one unit.

Example: 6 books cost ₹480. Find the cost of 8 books.

  1. Cost per book = ₹480 ÷ 6 = ₹80.
  2. Cost of 8 books = 8 × ₹80 = ₹640.
Direct proportion: quantity doubles, cost doubles

Another example: A car travels 432 km using 48 litres of petrol. At the same fuel efficiency, distance for 20 litres = 432 × 20 ÷ 48 = 180 km.

07

Inverse proportion

When one quantity increases, the other decreases so their product stays constant.

Opposite direction

Examples: more workers need fewer days for the same job; more identical pumps need less time to fill the same tank.

x × y = k

For two pairs of values:

x₁ × y₁ = x₂ × y₂

Example: 3 pumps fill a tank in 12 hours. How long for 6 pumps?

  1. Use pumps × hours = constant.
  2. 3 × 12 = 6 × x.
  3. x = 36 ÷ 6 = 6 hours.
More pumps → less time
3 pumps
12 hours
⬇️
6 pumps
6 hours
Do not assume “one goes up and the other goes down” is enough by itself. It is inverse proportion only when the product stays constant under the stated conditions.
08

Workers, time, food and pipes

These word problems test whether students can identify the relationship.

A. Workers and days

10 men dig a trench in 6 hours. How many men are needed to do the same job in 12 hours?

Men × hours = constant → 10 × 6 = x × 12 → x = 5 men.

B. Food and number of people

Food lasts 120 people for 200 days. After 5 days, 30 people leave. How long will the remaining food last for 90 people?

  1. Food remaining after 5 days = 120 × 195 person-days.
  2. For 90 people, remaining days = (120 × 195) ÷ 90 = 260 days.

This assumes each person consumes food at the same daily rate and the original supply was exactly sufficient for 120 people for 200 days.

C. Work done together

If Aman completes a job in 2 hours, his rate is 1/2 job per hour. If Rohan completes it in 2.5 hours, his rate is 1/2.5 = 2/5 job per hour.

Together: 1/2 + 2/5 = 9/10 job per hour. Time = 1 ÷ (9/10) = 10/9 hours (1 hour 6 minutes 40 seconds).

For combined work, add the work rates first, then divide one whole job by the combined rate.
09

Common mistakes to prevent

Use this checklist while students solve on the board.

  • ☐ Comparing different units without converting them.
  • ☐ Reversing the order of a ratio.
  • ☐ Forgetting to add all ratio parts before splitting a total.
  • ☐ Multiplying only one term when creating an equivalent ratio.
  • ☐ Confusing direct proportion with inverse proportion.
  • ☐ Forgetting that a full pie chart is 360°.
  • ☐ Forgetting to convert cm to km for map questions.
  • ☐ Not checking whether the final shares add to the original total.
  • ☐ Treating every increase/decrease relationship as inverse without checking the product.
